> *Per-tech revenue estimates are based on ServiceTitan job counts × avg ticket. ST per-tech attribution can overstate individual numbers due to job reassignment (e.g., Tom: $119K ST vs $98K QB). Every one of these moves puts more money in the techs' pockets. Nobody earns less. Most earn significantly more.

This document includes a **quarterly execution plan** for each initiative with milestones, KPIs, and a review cadence so progress is measurable.

## How We Track This

### Quarterly Business Review (QBR)

Tracy prepares a one-page scorecard for each initiative before every quarterly review. Ken reviews the numbers, makes decisions, and sets direction for the next quarter. 15–20 minutes per quarter.

**Schedule:**

| Review | Date (approx.) | Covers |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| QBR 1 | Early July 2026 | Q2 results — first full quarter of execution |
| QBR 2 | Early October 2026 | Q3 results — mid-year check |
| QBR 3 | Early January 2027 | Q4 results — full year wrap-up |
| QBR 4 | Early April 2027 | Q1 2027 — year-over-year comparison |

**What Tracy prepares for each QBR:**
- One-page scorecard per initiative (template below)
- Revenue vs. target by division (ServiceTitan + QuickBooks cross-check)
- Any flags or decisions needed from Ken

**What Ken decides at each QBR:**
- Whether to adjust targets, timelines, or approach
- Whether coaching/mentoring is working or needs a different angle
- Resource allocation for the next quarter

### Monthly Pulse Check (Optional)

If Ken wants more frequent visibility, Tracy can pull a quick monthly snapshot — just the key numbers, no analysis. Five minutes. Available but not required.

### The Scorecard Template

Each initiative tracked with this format:

```
INITIATIVE: [Name]
QUARTER: Q_ 2026
TARGET: [what we said would happen]
ACTUAL: [what happened]
STATUS: On Track / Behind / Ahead
NEXT STEP: [one action for next quarter]
FLAG: [anything Ken needs to weigh in on]
```

Tracy fills these in from ServiceTitan + QuickBooks data before each QBR. Ken reviews and decides.

### Graduated Bonus Structure (Ken's Decision)

An option to consider — replace the flat 3% quarterly bonus with a tiered structure:

| Avg Jobs/Day | Bonus % | Est. Quarterly Payout |
|-------------:|--------:|----------------------:|
| < 3.0 | 0% | $0 |
| 3.0 – 3.4 | 1% | ~$1,000 |
| 3.5 – 3.9 | 2% | ~$2,000 |
| 4.0+ | 3% | ~$3,000 |

Most techs already hit 3.5+, so this changes nothing for them. It only adjusts the incentive for anyone leaving early. Combined with dispatch priority (full-day techs get first pick of premium jobs the next day), this costs nothing.

**This is Ken's call.** Tracy can prepare the data showing where each tech falls, but Ken decides whether and when to implement.

## What Makes This Growth Possible

Scaling from $2.74M to $3.2M+ with 12 techs instead of 9 means more of everything that keeps the business running:

- **More dispatch complexity** — 12 techs across two divisions, three in training at different skill levels
- **More scheduling** — ride-alongs, ramp schedules, balancing veteran and new tech loads
- **More collections and AR** — higher revenue means more invoices, more follow-ups
- **More onboarding support** — Collin, Robert, and Chris all need operational support through their ramp
- **More performance tracking** — weekly ticket reviews, quarterly scorecards, early issue flagging
- **More vendor/inventory management** — more trucks, more equipment, more supplies

## The Investment: Tracy's Compensation Adjustment

| | Current | Proposed |
|--|--:|--:|
| Base salary | $89,000 | $95,000 |
| Quarterly bonus | $2,500/qtr flat ($10,000/yr) | 0.35% of quarterly revenue (~$2,400/qtr, ~$9,600/yr) |
| **Total comp** | **$99,000** | **~$104,600** |
| **Increase** | | **~$5,600/year** |

Tracy's base has been $89,000 for 21 months — no adjustment despite 4.8% pricing increases that automatically raised tech compensation. The proposed adjustment covers the pricing catch-up. The quarterly bonus stays in the same dollar range as today (~$2,400 vs $2,500) — the only change is tying it to revenue so it keeps pace with pricing going forward.

$5,600 represents roughly **1.2% of the identified growth opportunity**. If even one of these four initiatives succeeds because the back office is running well, the adjustment pays for itself.
